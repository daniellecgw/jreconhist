Conference Mission

The Junior Economic History Conference is a small, seminar-style workshop dedicated to early career researchers in economic history. Each spring, ten to twelve untenured economic historians will present full-length papers across a weekend, with substantial time for discussion. The first edition will be hosted at Rhodes College in March 2027.

Eligbility to Apply

The conference is open to early-career scholars in economic history, primarily assistant professors and other reserachers within 10 years of their PhD completion, including post-docs. Submissions are welcome from R1, R2, liberal-arts, and government researchers across north America, and acceptance turns on the quality of the paper.

Format

Each paper receives a 60-minute slot, structured either as a full seminar or a 45-minute presentation with 15 minutes of dedicated discussion. Authors submit a full paper with their application. The conference runs across two days: a Friday-evening arrival, a full ady of sessions and dinner on Saturday, and a half-day of sessions on Sunday. The workshop is small; 10 to 12 participants total. Participants are required to read the introduction of all papers on the program before attending, and encouraged to read the drafts fully. The organizers do not present their own work.

Organizers

This conference is currently jointly organized by Pier Paolo Creanza (Swarthmore College), João Tampellini (Rhodes College), and Danielle Graves Williamson (Williams College).
